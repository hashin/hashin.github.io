We have debated much on what makes love, *love* — and also what makes it so beautiful. For many years, I was content with the simple explanations that concerned themselves with the biology and the science around it. Since then, a world of literature and art has flowed through me. As years go by, accreting nostalgia adds a layer of incredulity to everything that we otherwise consider as authentic and unequivocally true. Even our *registers of emotions,* once lived ***fully,*** slowly subside and lose character. As memory slowly but certainly discards pieces we once held safely inside our hearts, we realise that love, just like everything human, *shifts shape* within us. 

At this stage, it is almost impossible for me to just explain away the *beauty* with science. This is not because science is *inadequate*. Rather, it stems from a desire to develop frameworks that would help us explain most — if not all — people we come across us in our lives. Their *love* is no less than ours and their experiences are *valid*. To write them is to *understand* them and to satiate this perverse desire to *write people* with *love*, one needs to explore this facet of the human condition.

One of the biggest *allures* of love is the spectre of *losing it fully*. If there is no uncertainty, love fails to captivate. Even in the most *mundane* of domestic settings, if a cascade of events pushes things to a precarious precipice, *love* is suddenly back in action! It is almost as if being on tenterhooks could save any relationship from turning stale. 

After all, what is love if we *don't fear* losing it?

Modern discourse could perhaps scoff at this obsession with uncertainty and dub it toxic (perhaps rightly so). But it is *almost impossible* to celebrate love without it. The longing, the shadow of loss, the confusion — all of this is necessary to push the human mind into a stupor, which is required to *tolerate the absurdities* that accompany love.

And hence, the mind should keep asking — *Anjaam Hamara, Kya Hoga?* [What will be our fate?]

Repeating this *desperate, ominous* plea exposes the way love shakes the foundations of a stability that the human mind so hopelessly desires. This *stability* is synonymous with death, much like a thermodynamic equilibrium equates to biological death. Love is an antidote to death not because it shuns mortality, but becuase it is *life itself*. Love cannot live in the same sentence that sports *certainty*.

Now listen to this loverboi passionately asking his beloved about their fate. He should know that he won't feel half the *pain* if his question is answered. And losing that *pain* will steal the *charm*, his *beloved*, his *love*, and ultimately *himself* from him.  That would be the end of love.

To be in love is to hopelessly wander across these *unsure* realms till one is completely lost.

Sufis of all hues will agree. They could never see their shapeless *beloved*, with a large ocean of light and dark sky preventing them from merging with their *beloved*. And they chose to *seek, and not just believe,* eliminating the need to bring any sort of stability into their chaotic lives.

Therefore, they never saw their beloved. The beloved was always a step, a hand, a breath, a dream, a whisper, a sujood, and [an ocean of wujud](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/umar-khayyam/) away. And it is impossible to *corrupt* that kind of love.

Enough for now. I will let this song stir you further away from certainty and towards the *shores of love*.
